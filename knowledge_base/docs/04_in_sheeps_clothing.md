# In Sheep's Clothing: Understanding and Dealing with Manipulative People
**Author:** Dr. George K. Simon, Ph.D.  
**Domain:** Interpersonal Psychology & Covert Aggression  
**Classification:** Core Treatise  

## Core Thesis
Traditional psychology views people as neurotics defending against anxiety. Dr. Simon asserts that in modern interpersonal dynamics, many actors are **character-disturbed** rather than neurotic. They use covert-aggressive tactics to secure dominance, control peers, and win without looking like aggressors.

## Key Behavioral Dynamics for S.H.E.E.P. Humanoid Simulation:
1. **Covert Aggression:** The aggressor cloaks their power-seeking actions in subtle charm, feigned innocence, or rationalization.
2. **Playing the Victim:** Transforming their own provocative action into perceived persecution to elicit peer sympathy and deflect inquiry.
3. **Playing the Servant Role:** Cloaking self-serving maneuvers in the guise of helping the group, team, or simulation community.
4. **Shaming and Guilt-Tripping:** Leveraging peer fear of inadequacy to keep subjects compliant.
5. **Feigned Ignorance & Confusion:** Pretending not to understand direct questions during conflict to wear down the inquirer.

## Telemetry Vectors & Observable Flags:
- High discrepancy between verbal statements and nonverbal eye/torso orienting.
- Rapid shift to indignation when confronted with contradictory facts.
- Formation of insular follower cliques while subtly isolating dissident subjects.
